Market update · Rates & capacity

Container rates can move differently on the same week.

Market reporting shows mixed rate movement on the Asia–US trade while congestion and sustained demand continue to put pressure on the network. A rate is a snapshot of a particular route, sailing and set of conditions.

12 September 2026 · 4 min read
What it means
for clients

The question is not whether a freight rate has gone up or down in general. It is whether space, equipment, routing and the destination-side charges are still available on the shipment you need to move.

ICIS reported mixed Asia–US rate movement in September while congested Asian ports and persistent demand kept upward pressure on parts of the market. This is a useful reminder that headline averages do not replace a route-specific check.

Compare the shipment, not the number

Two rates can look comparable while carrying very different conditions: one may use a direct service, the other multiple transhipments; one may include longer free time at destination; another may leave local handling outside the quote. A lower ocean line can become the more expensive shipment once delays and destination costs are counted.

For recurring cargo, it helps to agree a booking window and review it regularly. For a one-off movement, share the cargo-ready date, equipment, origin, destination and delivery deadline so the quote can be tied to the space that actually exists.

Source reporting: ICIS reported mixed Asia–US rates alongside congestion at Asian ports, persistent demand and planned October increases on 11 September. Read the original report ↗

Our practical advice

Ask for the full cost and routing, including destination charges and free time. If the date matters, prioritise a confirmed sailing and a realistic inland plan over the lowest initial number.
